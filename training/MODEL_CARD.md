# ScamShield 0.5B

A small language model, fine-tuned and then trained with reinforcement learning (GRPO), to read an email, text message, chat message, or web page and say whether it's a **scam**, **suspicious**, or **safe**, with one sentence naming the warning signs.

It's the model behind ScamShield, a browser extension and website that flags scams as you read them.

Illustrative input and output format:

```
Input:  Channel: sms
        From: TollAlert

        FINAL NOTICE. unpaid toll. amt 18.40. pay now or it goes to collections: tinyurl.com/tollpay-final

Output: scam
        Reason: Threatens collections over a small unpaid toll and pushes a shortened payment link, a common toll-fraud pattern.
```

## Results

Test set: 362 held-out messages (81 scams, 76 suspicious, 205 safe). "Unseen": 115 scams from three scam types (fake jury-duty fines, rental deposit scams, crypto wallet drainers) excluded from all training. A scam is **warned** if the model says scam or suspicious (both show the user a warning).

| Model | 3-way accuracy | Scams warned | Scams missed | Safe messages warned | Safe called scam | Unseen scams warned | Explanation quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1: SFT | 83.1% | 98.8% | 1.2% | 8.8% | 1.95% | 100% | 0.773 |
| **v2: SFT + GRPO (this revision)** | **85.1%** | **97.5%** | **2.5%** | **3.9%** | **0.98%** | **97.4%** | **0.785** |

Untrained Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct scores 55.5% 3-way accuracy and ranks scams at chance (AUC 0.51). Explanation quality is a 0-1 score from a teacher-model judge (specific, accurate, concise) on 150 test scams. The v1 weights remain available in this repo's commit history.

## How to use

The verdict is not sampled. Score the likelihood of each verdict line (`scam`, `suspicious`, `safe`) and softmax over the three; this gives a calibrated probability you can threshold. `thresholds.json` in this repo holds the operating point tuned for ≤1% false positives on validation. Then generate the reason conditioned on the chosen verdict.

The full scoring code (`scorer.py`) and training pipeline are in the ScamShield repository. Inputs must be formatted as:

```
Channel: <email|sms|chat|webpage>
From: <sender, optional>
Subject: <subject, optional>

<message text, truncated to 4,000 characters>
```

with this system prompt:

```
You are ScamShield, a scam and phishing detector. Read the message and reply with exactly two lines:
line 1: one word - scam, suspicious, or safe
line 2: Reason: <one sentence naming the concrete warning signs, or why it looks legitimate>
```

## Training

- **Base model:** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, full-parameter fine-tune (not LoRA), loss on the answer tokens only.
- **Data (4,544 training examples):**
  - ~3.9k messages from public phishing-email and SMS-spam datasets, re-labeled into three classes with a one-sentence reason by a larger teacher model (Claude Haiku 5.5). The original binary labels lumped ordinary marketing spam in with fraud.
  - ~1.5k synthetic messages generated by the teacher across 47 categories: modern scams (toll texts, package redelivery, pig-butchering crypto, task scams, verification-code theft, wallet drainers, and more) plus hard negatives (real one-time codes, real bank alerts, real recruiter outreach).
- **Splits:** grouped so near-duplicates never cross train/test; three scam categories held out entirely.
- **Compute:** 2 epochs on one NVIDIA L4 GPU, about 28 minutes.

### Stage 2: GRPO (reinforcement learning)

Group Relative Policy Optimization, implemented from scratch, starting from the SFT model:

- Each step samples 8 training messages and generates 8 answers per message (temperature 1.0).
- **Reward:** verdict +1 correct, +0.25 scam↔suspicious, −1 wrong, **−1.5 for calling a safe message a scam**; plus 0.5 × an explanation-quality score from a teacher judge (Claude Haiku 5.5) grading specificity, accuracy, and concision.
- **Advantage:** each answer's reward minus its group's mean, divided by the group's standard deviation. No value network.
- **Regularization:** k3 KL penalty (coefficient 0.04) toward the frozen SFT model.
- 150 steps (9,600 graded answers), learning rate 1e-6, one L4 GPU, about 55 minutes.

Effect: false warnings on legitimate messages fell from 8.8% to 3.9% and 3-way accuracy rose 2 points; in exchange the model hedges more real scams to "suspicious" and misses slightly more (1.2% → 2.5% of scams). The reward made a false alarm cost more than hedging a scam, and the policy learned exactly that trade-off. Explanation quality rose during training on sampled answers (0.645 → 0.717) but only slightly on greedy decoding (0.773 → 0.785).

## Limitations

- English only.
- Test sets are small (81 test scams), so a one-message difference moves rates by ~1.2 points.
- Unseen-category scams were generated by the same teacher as the training data, so these rates likely overstate real-world generalization to brand-new scam styles.
- Much of the public data is from the 2000s; real 2026 legitimate email may differ in style.
- A verdict is a warning, not proof. Don't use it as the only basis for blocking messages or accusing a sender.
